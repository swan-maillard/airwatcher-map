AirWatcher

A C++ console app we wrote in our third year at INSA Lyon. It reads a year of readings from 100 air-quality sensors, finds the ones that look faulty and checks whether two air cleaners did anything.

What you're looking at

Each dot is a sensor from the project's data set: 100 sensors on a 10 by 10 grid between 44° and 47.6° north, each giving one reading a day through 2019 for four pollutants. Those are ozone (O₃), sulphur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) and fine particles (PM10), all in µg/m³. The two squares are air cleaners. There's no base map, the dots are placed from their latitude and longitude.

The scan I wrote looks for sensors that disagree with the sensors around them. For each sensor, it takes the five closest other sensors and their last readings. From those it builds, per pollutant, a reference (a mean) and a tolerance (the variance, a measure of how spread out the neighbours are). If the sensor's own reading is further from the reference than the tolerance for any of the four pollutants, it's flagged as probably faulty. On this data it flags 6 of the 100 sensors.

The air-cleaner check compares each sensor's reading just before a cleaner was switched on with its reading just before it was switched off. A drop of 25% or more in at least one pollutant counts as an effect. It walks outwards from the cleaner, sensor by sensor, and the distance to the last sensor that still shows an effect is the cleaner's radius. Sensors flagged by the scan are left out.
